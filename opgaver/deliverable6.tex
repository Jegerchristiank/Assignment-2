\subsection{Deliverable 6: Part II Reflection Questions}
\label{sec:deliverable6}

\paragraph*{Notation and framing}
In Part II, \textbf{RQ} denotes \textit{research question} (RQ1-RQ3), and \textbf{M} denotes one selected assessment \textit{method} from the toolkit (e.g., M2, M10). The report uses \textit{TAT} as shorthand for \textit{turnaround time}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Initial exploration and relevance to the three research questions.}  
The dataset contains 150 events, 17 cases, 14 columns, and 12 activity types across 2026-01-12 07:45 to 2026-01-13 08:46. Core workflow fields are present, while context-heavy fields are sparse (e.g., \texttt{study\_uid}, \texttt{series\_count}, \texttt{instance\_count}, \texttt{icd11\_code}). Following the \textit{Five Facets} framework, this means the dataset is stronger for \textit{Task/System} sequence analysis than for rich \textit{Data/Source/Human} interpretation \parencite{mohammed2025fivefacets}. Applied to this case, the file can support RQ1 (workflow efficiency and \textit{TAT} bottlenecks), RQ2 (\textit{conformance} for critical communication), and RQ3 (\textit{representativity} and workload patterns for modelling), but with explicit limits on contextual inference.

\item \textbf{Method selection justification and trade-offs.}  
M2, M6, M8 (15 min), M10, and M11 were selected as a \textit{coverage portfolio}: together they cover all three RQs with low redundancy across facets. The completed selection table is documented in Appendix~\ref{app:part2_supporting}, Table~\ref{tab:d6_method_selection}. Non-selected methods (M1, M3, M4, M5, M7, M9) were excluded because they either duplicated information already produced by selected methods or targeted narrower issues that did not improve RQ coverage enough. The core trade-off was breadth across \textit{facets} versus depth in fewer dimensions. RQ1 weighted timing integrity higher (therefore M10), RQ2 weighted communication safety higher (therefore M8), and RQ3 weighted representativity/workload structure higher (therefore M6 and M11).

\item \textbf{Results interpretation by method.}  
Chapter 27 explains that \textit{process mining} uses temporal event sequences to reveal common pathways and frequent exceptions \parenciteshort{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27}. Applied here, Appendix~\ref{app:part2_supporting}, Table~\ref{tab:d6_method_results} shows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item M2: high context missingness (e.g., \texttt{study\_uid} 134/150), so flow-level findings are feasible but context-sensitive claims are weaker; expected and coherent with synthetic-log design.
  \item M6: urgency and shift imbalance (15 stat/urgent vs 2 routine; Day 11, Night 6, Evening 0), so \textit{representativity} for broad generalisation is limited; expected and relevant for RQ3 risk.
  \item M8: 16 pass and 1 fail on critical communication; overall \textit{conformance} is high, but the single fail remains operationally important for RQ2; method behaved as expected.
  \item M10: 6/17 cases with \textit{batch-logging} signatures and a large raw-vs-clean TAT gap (147.93 vs 198.50 min), making timestamp credibility a central RQ1 risk; this was the most surprising result.
  \item M11: uneven actor distribution (radiographer 64 events, resident 7, system actor concentration), indicating workload concentration affecting interpretation; expected and useful for RQ1/RQ3 context.
\end{itemize}
Possible improvements are stricter timestamp validation rules for M10 and role-normalised comparators for M11, to reduce overinterpretation from small samples.

\item \textbf{Proposed DQ dimension.}  
The proposed dimension is \textit{Batch Logging Credibility}: whether timestamp structure reflects real-time documentation or delayed/post-hoc entry. The motivating pattern is repeated timestamps and tight clustering in cases such as CXR-013 and CXR-011. In this context, low credibility biases RQ1 (\textit{TAT} and bottlenecks), can mask RQ2 communication delay, and can leak artefactual timing patterns into RQ3 modelling. The custom method code is included in Appendix~\ref{app:d6_custom_code}, and the facet mapping is documented in Appendix~\ref{app:part2_supporting}, Table~\ref{tab:d6_custom_facet_mapping}.

\item \textbf{Per-facet data quality synthesis.}  
The custom method code is documented in Appendix~\ref{app:d6_custom_code}. It scores per-case timestamp risk from duplicate-time frequency, short-window event density, and low unique-timestamp ratio.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Data}: missing context fields and timestamp artefacts reduce precision; consequence is weaker causal interpretation in RQ1 and RQ2.
  \item \textit{Source}: mixed real-time and delayed logging reduces reliability; consequence is uncertainty about what truly happened when.
  \item \textit{System}: safety-relevant communication events can be missing despite completed workflow; consequence is false reassurance for RQ2.
  \item \textit{Task}: skewed representativity limits external validity for optimisation/triage; consequence is weak transferability for RQ1 and RQ3.
  \item \textit{Human}: tacit coordination is weakly represented in logs; consequence is underestimation of practical workload and exception handling.
\end{itemize}
Most challenging facet is \textit{Human}. With the available methods, tacit judgement and informal handoff logic are hard to assess. A stronger design would combine log analytics with targeted qualitative validation (short observations and focused interviews around critical cases).

\item \textbf{Invisible work and system design implications.}  
The completed invisible-work table is in Appendix~\ref{app:part2_supporting}, Table~\ref{tab:d6_invisible_work}. For each activity, the non-logging reasons combine \textit{technical} constraints (events happen outside core systems), \textit{social} factors (verbal/ad hoc coordination), and \textit{economic} constraints (documentation burden under time pressure). A model based only on log traces therefore overestimates formal linearity and underestimates coordination work.  

Consequences by RQ are direct: RQ1 may misclassify bottlenecks, RQ2 may underestimate communication-risk pathways, and RQ3 may train on simplified patterns that ignore real coordination load. This directly parallels Part I, where interview and observation produced a structured \textit{say-do gap}. In both parts, \textit{work-as-recorded} is valuable but incomplete.  

In Chapter 9 terms, this is a \textit{formalisation continuum} problem: complete formalisation is neither realistic nor always desirable \parencite{guidehealthinformatics2026ch9and27}. This aligns with declarative workflow thinking, where robust support balances \textit{flexibility} and \textit{constraints} instead of enforcing one rigid path \parencite{vanderaalst2009declarative}. A concrete design change is lightweight optional contextual events (consultation, reprioritisation reason, communication intent) with low-friction UI defaults, periodic review, and clear limits on secondary use to mitigate documentation burden and surveillance effects.
\end{enumerate}
