\subsection{Deliverable 1: Description of the Routine Based on the Interview}
\label{sec:deliverable1}

The study was conducted individually with one participant (the author). The chosen routine is a weekday morning routine.

The interview was conducted on February 18, 2026 as a self-recorded, open-question interview. Following \textcite{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27}, this step is treated as \textit{manual knowledge acquisition}: it captures \textit{knowledge-as-stated} and a first \textit{work-as-imagined} model of the routine \parenciteshort{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27,zajac2026methodsknowing}.

The routine boundary is defined as start when the alarm sounds and end when the apartment door closes, based directly on transcript lines L21-L22 in Appendix~\ref{app:part1_transcript}, Table~\ref{tab:app_transcript_part1}. The baseline sequence (wake-up, hygiene, dressing/packing, breakfast, checks, departure) is grounded in lines L02-L12, and variation points (snooze, breakfast substitution, key search, occasional roommate coordination) are grounded in lines L14-L18.

Routine boundary:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Start: alarm sounds.
  \item End: participant leaves home (door closes).
\end{itemize}

The full interview-based activity table is documented in Appendix~\ref{app:part1_supporting}, Table~\ref{tab:d1_activity_spec}. Figure~\ref{fig:d1_dcr_graph} shows the corresponding \textit{interview-based DCR} model exported from DCR Graph. The graph was built from activities A01-A09 and used as the baseline representation for comparison against the observed event log in Deliverables 2 and 3.

Assumptions used for modelling:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The model represents a weekday baseline (not weekend behavior).
  \item The participant is the only consistent human actor in the routine.
  \item Expected times are interview estimates and are treated as approximate.
\end{itemize}

These assumptions are made explicit to support direct comparison between \textit{knowledge-as-stated} and the observed data in Deliverables 2 and 3.
