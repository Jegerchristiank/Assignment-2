\subsubsection{Missing Events}
The missing-event category is represented by D01. The interview model expects a distinct seated breakfast-and-schedule-review step (A06), but this did not occur as a separable observed event. Instead, nutrition was reduced to a quick banana and coffee transfer. This shows that a declared routine element can be absent in practice without aborting the overall routine.

\subsubsection{Extra Events}
D02 captures an event absent from the interview baseline: a short roommate-message response at 06:52. Although brief, it consumed time early in the routine and influenced subsequent timing. This illustrates that event logs can expose routine ``noise'' that interview participants may not consider worth reporting.

\subsubsection{Different Order}
D03 shows a sequence inversion: bag packing (A04) occurred after breakfast preparation had started (E07, E09), while the interview sequence placed packing before breakfast (L05-L07). This is a concrete say-do gap in ordering, not merely in timing.

\subsubsection{Timing Distortions}
D04 documents both delay and compression effects. Wake-up actions were delayed by snoozing and phone checking, while the hygiene block was executed faster than the interview estimate. Timing distortions therefore occurred in opposite directions within the same routine.

\subsubsection{Workarounds and Coordination Steps}
D05 demonstrates an ad hoc coordination step: borrowing a lock through text when a needed item was unavailable. The interview did mention that rare interactions can happen (L18), but not this specific dependency path. The workaround was critical for completion and would be invisible in a purely normative process description.

\subsubsection{Relabeling and Ambiguity}
D06 concerns category boundaries. The interview label ``breakfast'' implied a sit-down meal; observation produced a split pattern (banana + coffee-to-go). This makes it unclear whether breakfast should be encoded as preparation, consumption, or a combined concept. Without explicit coding rules, multiple interpretations remain valid.

\subsubsection{Summary of Key Differences and Implications}
All six required delta categories are present and traceable. The comparison shows that the interview model is useful as a high-level skeleton but underrepresents contingent actions, local reordering, and category ambiguity. The observed log improves empirical grounding but introduces its own modelling challenge: event labels must be tightly defined to avoid interpretive drift. Together, Deliverables 1-3 provide the evidence base for reflective analysis in Deliverable 4.
