\subsection{Deliverable 3: Delta Sheet Summarising Differences Between Stated and Observed Practice}
\label{sec:deliverable3}

\subsubsection{Comparison Method and Mapping Criteria}
The comparison maps interview activities (A01-A09; Table~\ref{tab:d1_activity_spec}) to observed events (E01-E14; Table~\ref{tab:d2_observed_excerpt}). A delta was recorded when presence/absence, order, timing, coordination behavior, or label interpretation differed. In assignment terms, this is a structured comparison of knowledge-as-stated and knowledge-as-discovered.

Two decision rules were used for consistency. First, a timing distortion was registered when observed start/end placement shifted by more than three minutes relative to the expected D1 window. Second, relabeling/ambiguity was registered when one interview label could validly map to more than one observed operational interpretation.

\subsubsection{Missing Events}
D01 captures a missing event: A06 (seated breakfast plus schedule review) did not occur as a distinct observed event. Instead, nutrition was reduced to banana + coffee-to-go. A stated routine element was therefore absent without preventing completion.

\subsubsection{Extra Events}
D02 captures an extra event: a short roommate-message response at 06:52. Although brief, it affected early timing and was not part of the interview baseline.

\subsubsection{Different Order}
D03 shows a sequence inversion: bag packing (A04) occurred after breakfast preparation had started (E07, E09), while the interview sequence placed packing before breakfast (L05-L07). This is a direct say-do gap in ordering.

\subsubsection{Timing Distortions}
D04 shows a phase shift. Wake-up actions were delayed (snooze + phone check), so hygiene started later than the expected window even though duration was near the lower bound. The key issue is shifted placement, not only speed.

\subsubsection{Workarounds and Coordination Steps}
D05 identifies an ad hoc workaround: borrowing a lock by text when a needed item was missing. The interview mentioned rare interaction (L18), but not this concrete dependency path. This step was essential for successful completion.

\subsubsection{Relabeling and Ambiguity}
D06 concerns label ambiguity. In the interview, ``breakfast'' implied a sit-down meal; in observation it became banana + coffee-to-go. This blurs whether ``breakfast'' should be coded as preparation, consumption, or both.

\subsubsection{Summary of Key Differences and Implications}
All six required delta categories are present and traceable (Table~\ref{tab:d3_delta_counts}). The interview model provides a clear high-level skeleton, but it underrepresents contingency, local reordering, and label ambiguity. The observed log adds empirical grounding but requires stricter label definitions. Together, Deliverables 1-3 provide a clear evidence chain for Deliverable 4.
