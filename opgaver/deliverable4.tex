\subsection{Deliverable 4: Reflections on the Modelling Process}
\label{sec:deliverable4}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Data missed or misrepresented in the interview-based model.}  
The interview model captured routine intent, but missed several situated details that appeared in observation: micro-coordination (E03), recovery behavior (E10), and omission of the planned seated breakfast-and-schedule step. This difference is expected in \textit{manual knowledge acquisition}: \textit{interviews} mainly capture recollected intent, while \textit{observation} captures situated adaptation \parenciteshort{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27,zajac2026methodsknowing}.

\item \textbf{What counted as an activity in each model.}  
In Deliverable 1, the \textit{interview-based DCR} uses intention-level activities (A01-A09). In Deliverable 2, the event log (the \textit{observed DSR}) uses timestamped observable actions. The granularity mismatch means one DCR activity can map to multiple logged events. This is why the same routine can look stable at intention level and variable at event level.

\item \textbf{How the event log constrained what could be modelled.}  
The event log supports strong analysis of order, timing, and deviations (\textit{work-as-recorded}), but it captures motives and judgement only indirectly. Without contextual notes, interpretation of why a deviation happened remains weak. This is why descriptive/reflexive note separation was retained in the method and documented in Appendix~\ref{app:part1_supporting}, Table~\ref{tab:d2_observation_guide}.

\item \textbf{Potential and limits of automatic event capture.}  
Automatic capture is strong for digital traces (alarm, app checks, timestamps), but weaker for tacit coordination, local workarounds, and searches for missing items. In Chapter 27 terms, automated traces should be combined with \textit{qualitative discovery} methods when the task includes social and contextual adaptation \parenciteshort{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27,zajac2026methodsknowing}.

\item \textbf{Variations and exceptions revealed by observation.}  
Observation revealed concrete \textit{exceptions}: changed order (E07 before E09), omitted planned step (A06 not distinct), and workaround behavior (E10). Applied to this case, the source logic is that process evidence should not only test conformance, but also explain recurring exceptions that may be locally rational \parenciteshort{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27}.

\item \textbf{Relevance of qualitative discovery in process modelling.}  
For this routine, model quality improved by triangulating \textit{knowledge-as-stated} (interview + transcript), \textit{knowledge-as-discovered} (observation), and \textit{work-as-recorded} (event log). The result is a more realistic routine model than any single method alone, consistent with Chapter 27's iterative \textit{knowledge acquisition} approach \parenciteshort{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27}.
\end{enumerate}
