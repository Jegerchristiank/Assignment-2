\subsection{Deliverable 4: Reflections on the Modelling Process}
\label{sec:deliverable4}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Missed or misrepresented data}
\par\textbf{Answer}\par
\textbf{Claim.} The interview-based model captured intent, but underrepresented situated coordination and recovery behaviour.

\textbf{Evidence.} Observation added micro-coordination (E03), workaround behaviour (E10), and omission of the planned seated breakfast-and-schedule step.

\textbf{Implication.} Interview-only modelling overstates routine linearity and should be treated as a starting model, not a full process description.

\item \textbf{Activity definition across models}
\par\textbf{Answer}\par
\textbf{Claim.} The two models use different granularity.

\textbf{Evidence.} Deliverable 1 uses intention-level activities (Axx), while Deliverable 2 logs timestamped observable events (Exx).

\textbf{Implication.} One interview activity can map to multiple observed events, so apparent stability at intention level can hide operational variation.

\item \textbf{Event-log modelling constraints}
\par\textbf{Answer}\par
\textbf{Claim.} The event log supports sequence and timing analysis well, but limits interpretation of motives and local judgement.

\textbf{Evidence.} The log records \textit{what} happened and \textit{when}, while causal context is only partially recoverable from notes.

\textbf{Implication.} Process modelling from logs alone can identify deviations, but cannot fully explain why deviations occurred.

\item \textbf{Potential for automated capture}
\par\textbf{Answer}\par
\textbf{Claim.} Automatic capture is feasible for digital traces but incomplete for social and tacit work.

\textbf{Evidence.} Timestamped app events can be captured reliably, but ad hoc coordination and workaround decisions are often off-system.

\textbf{Implication.} Automated logging should be combined with targeted qualitative follow-up when model validity depends on context.

\item \textbf{Observed variations and exceptions}
\par\textbf{Answer}\par
\textbf{Claim.} Observation exposed exceptions that were absent from the stated routine.

\textbf{Evidence.} The observed trace showed order changes (E07 before E09), a missing distinct A06 step, and workaround handling (E10).

\textbf{Implication.} Conformance checking should be paired with exception analysis, because recurring local deviations can be practically rational.

\item \textbf{Role of qualitative discovery methods}
\par\textbf{Answer}\par
\textbf{Claim.} Qualitative methods improve model validity by grounding formal traces in real practice.

\textbf{Evidence.} The combined use of interview, observation, and event log produced a more coherent account than any single source alone.

\textbf{Implication.} Robust process models require triangulation across stated, observed, and recorded evidence \parenciteshort{guidehealthinformatics2026ch27,zajac2026methodsknowing}.
\end{enumerate}
